# Port status: QuickShell → pleamar

What of the legacy shell in `files/quickshell` has a counterpart in this port, and what has not.
Written because "the capsules are glass" was asked for twice and delivered late: a list makes the
gaps visible before they are asked about again.

**How this was made.** The inventory is the QML tree itself (`Bar/Modules`, `Components`,
`Services`, the top-level areas, and the two big trees `Chaldea/` and `greeter/`). A counterpart is
a scene (`shells/*`), a plugin (`lib/plugins/*`) or a library
(`lib/{capsule,icons,menu,palette}.plm`); evidence is the file headers that name their legacy
(`// Legacy: …`), `docs/INTERFACES.md`, and the behaviour itself. Statuses are by behaviour, not by
line: "ported" means the thing works in the port, not that its QML was translated one-to-one.

**Legend**

|             |                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **ported**  | a counterpart exists and its behaviour is there                                                                                                           |
| **partial** | a counterpart exists with a known gap (named in the note)                                                                                                 |
| **absent**  | nothing in the port                                                                                                                                       |
| **engine**  | the QML object is plumbing the scene language replaces outright (a behaviour, a layout host, a texture helper); there is nothing to port object by object |

### The tray's menu, and where it comes from

**Settled 2026-10-26: the engine reads it, no plugin is needed.** `bar.luau` asks
`sys.ask("tray.menu", key)` when the right button lands on an icon, and the engine answers the
item's own `com.canonical.dbusmenu` tree — `get tm.count` was 8 for vicinae («Toggle Vicinae»,
«Vicinae v0.29.0 [unknown]», a separator, «About Vicinae», «Settings…», …) and for Steam its five
recent games, Shop and Library, drawn on the bar's `barmenu` surface (screenshot, 2026-10-26). When
the ask fails, the logic falls back to `tray.context`, the application's own menu.

The earlier plan here — a `lib/plugins/traymenu` running `busctl` and parsing the layout — was
written for 0.4.11, where the record's `menu` arrived empty. The evidence it rested on still holds
and is worth keeping, because it is what the engine has to cope with: an item's menu is
`com.canonical.dbusmenu` at the path its `Menu` property names, the signature differs by
implementation (Steam takes `iias` with the old `parentId` as `i`, the spec's newer form is `uias`;
vicinae answered "No such method `GetLayout`" at its `/MenuBar` on 2026-10-24), and activation is
`com.canonical.dbusmenu.Event(id, "clicked", …)`.

What is left is depth and room: the tree arrives flat, and the menu draws at most 8 rows in its
300×176 surface.

## The short version

Ported, in the sense that the thing works: the bar and all its capsules, the launcher, notifications
(cards), overlay, overview, the drawer pages, the plugins (cover, meta, mpd, pill, spectrum,
genelec), the palette and its scripts, and the login/lock card.

Absent outright: `SubmenuHost`'s search field inside a submenu, the number-roll animations, and
Chaldea's 340 art files (by design: the behaviour lives in `panels/pages/media.plm` and
`shells/lock`).

Still partial, with the gap named in the tables below: the number-roll animations and the bar's
slide reveals (a text cannot roll a digit), the media capsule's transport reveal and the hover-open
delay the port refuses on purpose, the settings surface's keys the compiler refuses (`height`,
`animations`, `startupClean`) and its widget order, the `SunIcon` over the weather card's
conditions, the media tooltip's track quality (it reads a plugin's boundary — see «The plugin
boundary does not reach the scene's logic»), and in the lock tree the fingerprint PAM path, user and
session selection, the nixie lab and the greeter's nixie/CRT variants.

Closed since this paragraph was first written, so they are off the list: connectivity, weather
values and the notification history (2026-10-26/28); the tray's menus, their host, the audio
capsules and the workspace watcher (2026-10-31); the settings surface's `fontScale` and
`monitorScale`, the moon's drawn disc, the cover's remote art, one-overlay-at-a-time, the
now-playing popup, the greeter's theme tokens and the glyph shadow (2026-10-31, this session's
commits name each one).

Not to be ported for now, by the owner's word (2026-10-28): the nixie glass on the lock's clock. The
lock draws a slab, and that is where it stays until it is asked for again — the same call as the
`SubmenuHost` search field and the fingerprint reader.

### What is actually complete

Bar capsules and their geometry (clock, workspaces, keyboard, network, sysmon, media, volume, mic,
genelec, weather), the drawer pages, the notification server with history and DND, the toasts, the
screenshot card, the lock card and its PAM check, the overview scene, the plugins (cover, meta, mpd,
pill, spectrum, genelec), the palette pipeline and the theme scripts, the launcher (which the
reference does not have at all), and the six services pleamar itself provides (clock, audio,
network, media, workspaces/window, tray list, notifications).

The panels scene was the heaviest thing in the shell for the wrong reason: its catcher — the
full-monitor surface that dismisses a drawer on an outside press — stayed mapped for the life of the
session, so a 3840×2110 layer with an empty draw was painted at the screen's rate for a scene with
nothing on screen. What it cost was frames, not memory: **2423 frames in 10 s and 2 % of a core
before, 305 and 0 % after** (measured live on 2026-10-27, the same drawer opened and closed in both
runs; memory 79.8 MiB against 78.3, so a closed sheet keeps its buffers either way —
`tests/evidence/2026-10-27-panels.*`). The catcher now opens with the drawer it catches and closes
with it, a 1×1 keeper keeps the scene loaded, and the idle pace is 30 fps instead of 240 — 30 and
not 1, because the pace is also how long a `fact drawer …` waits to be drawn: at 1 fps the card took
**1.4 s** to appear, at 30 it takes ~40 ms.

One plugin fact outlives its scene: the Genelec mute. `state.json` carries `muted` beside `preMute`
since 2026-10-27, and `restore()` adopts it only together with a `preMute` to come back to. Verified
live: a press that mutes, `systemctl --user restart pleamar-bar`, a press that lifts it — the file
ends at `muted: false`, which is reachable only if the restored flag was `true`.

### The order the gaps fight each other in

1. The plugin boundary: a plugin's facts and texts are live for the renderer and stale for the
   scene's logic, which is what stops the media tooltip's quality line and the build badge's hover
   card. It is one decision — sync the boundary in the engine, or let each card ask its own source
   at hover time — and the owner's, not a scene's; the probes are written up above.
1. `SunIcon` over the weather card's conditions: **closed 2026-10-31**. The drawing is `lib/sun.plm`
   — a ring written as `repeat i in 0..71 { line ray.$i { … cos(0.0872665 * i) … } }` put all 72
   rays on one angle (`$i` reaches a name in this version, not an expression), so the per-ray
   constants are literals, generated once, with only `t` (`time`) left to vary — and it is placed:
   the weather card's corner, where the decor glyph sat, 72 px centred on (262, 52), at 22 %, one
   occupant for one slot (the sun for WMO 0-2, the condition's glyph for the rest). Walked headless
   in sway: the drawn ring, glow and core at three sizes, two shots two seconds apart differing in
   **1757 px** (it shimmers), and the card rendering both branches — sun at code 1, cloud at code 3
   — without either overlapping the moon row.
1. Everything else in the tables above, one at a time: `SubmenuHost`'s search field and the
   fingerprint decision, both the owner's, and the lock's nixie glass, which waits for the owner to
   ask for it again. The settings surface is closed as far as the compiler carries it.

### The plugin boundary does not reach the scene's logic (found 2026-10-31)

A plugin's facts and texts are live everywhere but in the scene's own Luau, and the bar's tooltip
cards read them there. Three probes on the running scene, each a read from outside (IPC) against the
card the logic builds:

| the scene shows                                                        | the logic read                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Pill.taken` true, `Pill.streak` 7                                     | the card said «Pill not taken yet»                        |
| `Build.count` 1, `Build.stage` building, `Build.summary` «fmt 2:20:13» | «Nix build: —», «State: idle · 0 running»                 |
| `Genelec.volume` -50, `Genelec.midi` true                              | «-40 dB», «host GLM adapter» — the declarations' defaults |

`pleamar --say bar 'fact Build.count 5'` was then read by the logic at once (the card said «5
running») and the plugin's logic put it back to 1 a moment later without the scene's logic noticing.
So the write direction works and the update direction does not: the plugin's `fact.count = …`
reaches the renderer and the IPC table, and not the scene's Luau mirror.

What it costs today: the pill, the MPD flags and the Genelec cards print the defaults their
libraries declare, and the build badge has no hover card (its plumbing was written and reverted
rather than shipped saying «idle» over a running build). The fix is either the engine — the plugin's
boundary update reaching the scene's logic — or four cards each asking its own source at hover time
(`nixbuild-status` for the badge, the pill and MPD their own). Not done here: it is one decision,
not four edits, and it is not a scene's to take.

### The lock as the engine runs it (measured 2026-10-27)

What stopped the lock was the engine, in two ways. The first is patched here; the second is open.

- **The engage was asked for before there was anything to ask with.** `src/render.rs:3526` engages
  the lock from the render loop once the surface's `open:` is true, and
  `src/platform/wayland.rs:893-897` skips lock surfaces while placing, on purpose — so a scene whose
  only surface is the lock leaves `placed` empty, and the `none of the monitors asked for …` warning
  that follows is about placement, not monitors. The render thread starts before the platform has
  made `LOCKS`, its one request was dropped silently and remembered as sent, and the loop then went
  to rest with the session unlocked: `WAYLAND_DEBUG=1` showed the manager bound and `lock` never
  sent. **Fixed** by `packages/overlays/pleamar-lock-engage.patch` — the request is remembered only
  once the platform took it, a refused one is asked again, and the platform sends
  `ToRender::Repaint` when it can take it. Verified on the live session:
  `lock · the session is locked`, `lock.held` reads 1, and the engine hands 3840×2160 frames to the
  compositor.
- **A lock the compositor ends crashes the scene — fixed 2026-10-27** (`dd83e555`): the release sent
  `unlock` on a proxy the compositor had already destroyed
  (`wl_display#1: error 0: invalid object 31`), the scene exited, and Hyprland answered the next
  locker with `lock · the compositor did not grant the lock, or ended it` — one session, one lock.
  `finished` now drops the bookkeeping it can no longer unlock, so the next `LockScreen(false)`
  finds nothing to release and the scene goes on. Walked 2026-10-28 on the real surface: **four
  engagements in one session**, each released by a password, and not one protocol error in the
  journal (`invalid object`, `Io error`, `did not grant` — none).
- **`describe` cannot see it.** `pleamar --say lock describe` answers `nothing on screen`, which is
  the protocol working: a `kind: lock` surface and `agent: hidden` are never told. A lock scene with
  no surface at all looks exactly as healthy from outside.
- **The password has a stack now.** `security.pam.services.pleamar` (fprintd `sufficient`, then
  `pam_unix`) writes `/etc/pam.d/pleamar`, which is the name the engine asks for. No fingerprint
  reader is plugged in, so every check logs
  `pam_fprintd(pleamar:auth): GetDevices failed: The name is not activatable` and falls through to
  the password — the line in the journal is the noise of the decision, not a fault. next switch. No
  fingerprint reader is plugged in, so the finger half falls through every time.
- **The frame cost, measured 2026-10-28 on the card's own scene.** `lock/preview.plm` — the same
  backdrop, clock and card, on a `kind: window` surface — ran at 3840×2110 against the real GPU
  (`AMD Radeon RX 9070 XT (RADV GFX1201)`), 240 Hz, scale 2, and `pleamar --report preview` measured
  20 s of it: **median 4.2 · p95 4.2 · p99 6.9 · worst 21.2 ms** (the screen gives 4.2), 64 late
  frames (1.3 %), paint 0.04 ms a frame, 1 % of a core, 59 MB RSS. The late frames are waiting for
  the screen (7.6 ms of 9.8 on average) with the GPU 33 % busy on other work, not the scene. The 8.6
  ms that the preview reported before was a headless run at 1280×720 **on llvmpipe**
  (`tests/shots/2026-10-16/preview.log`), which is not the path the lock takes. Not covered: the
  real `kind: lock` surface (fullscreen, every monitor, the wallpaper from settings), which needs a
